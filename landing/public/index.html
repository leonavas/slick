# welcome ★

Ultrafast, slim and beautiful Slack TUI.

One small Rust binary, no browser inside. It has all the important features and hotkeys from Slack.

# features
s
slick APP11:30

What the TUI does today:

  • Channels, private channels, DMs and group DMs. Start a new DM or channel with Ctrl+N.
  • Hotkeys for everything: Ctrl+K to jump to any conversation, j k to move, Tab to write, Shift+J K to reorder sections. Full list in .
  • Your Slack sidebar sections. Fold them, drag them, and the new order syncs back to Slack. Unread DMs float to the top.
  • Starred conversations, unread state and mention badges.
  • Search conversations by several words at once, with the matches highlighted.
  • Threads in a side panel, with “also send to channel”.
  • Reactions and a reaction picker, : emoji autocomplete, and your workspace's custom emoji, animated ones playing at the GIF's own pace.
  • Message actions: edit, delete, forward, save for later, remind me, mark unread, copy the link or the text, follow a thread.
  • Pinned messages, in their own tab in each conversation.
  • Avatars and images right in the conversation, in terminals with kitty, sixel or iTerm2 graphics (half blocks elsewhere). Click one to enlarge it; open or download shared files.
  • Block Kit and attachments drawn the way Slack draws them, with buttons you can click.
  • Your account: active or away, pause notifications, custom status, edit your profile. Other people's presence, status and profile cards.
  • Mouse support: drag to select messages and copy them, right-click menus, a sidebar you can resize.
  • A tray icon that lights up with unread DMs and mentions.
  • Updates itself from the GitHub releases, with one click.
  • Your terminal's own colors. Press t to see what that means.
  • In English or Brazilian Portuguese, switched in Settings.
  • Slack events for your system: new messages, mentions, reactions, presence, unread counts and pauses as a stream of JSON events, as hooks and as a state file, so any script, bar or notifier can react to them. Commands too: slick open, slick status, slick dnd.

# why-so-fast
y
you10:12

ok, but why is it so fast?

s
slick APP10:12

Rust, mostly. Here's my trust me bro benchmark: slick and the Slack desktop app, same two workspaces, running side by side on my machine right now:

                 slick    Slack
memory           73 MB    828 MB
processes        1        10
threads          6        124
install          10 MB    295 MB
cache on disk    21 MB    803 MB

Here's what makes that possible:

  • Native code, not a browser. slick is written in Rust and compiled ahead of time to machine code. No JavaScript engine to boot, no garbage collector pausing to clean up, nothing to warm up.
  • Light on memory. About 70 MB with my two workspaces open, against 830 MB spread over Slack's 10 processes. The terminal only redraws when something on screen changed.
  • Cache first. Users, channels, your recent conversations and their last 50 messages are saved locally, so they're on screen before Slack answers.
  • Never blocks. Every request runs on its own thread, and Rust guarantees those threads can't corrupt each other's data. Typing and scrolling never wait on the network.
  • One small binary. About 10 MB (Slack installs 295 MB), no runtime to install. No webview, no Electron. A browser window shows up only to sign in.
  • Asks for less. The open conversation checks for new messages every 3 seconds and only fetches what's newer than what you have.

# disk-usage
s
slick APP10:05

Slack's desktop app keeps growing on your disk. slick doesn't.

  • Only what's useful is cached. Users, channels, your 8 most recent conversations and their last 50 messages, in a single file.
  • Old images go away. Photos, avatars and emoji you haven't seen in 7 days are deleted when slick starts. What you keep looking at stays.

No more bloated disk space for a chat app. On my machine, right now:

$ du -sh ~/.cache/slick ~/.config/Slack
21M   ~/.cache/slick
803M  ~/.config/Slack

slick APP12:30

slick fits right into Omarchy.

It opens like any other Omarchy TUI app: from the launcher, in its own window, with its own icon. And it follows your Omarchy theme: slick draws with your terminal's colors, so when you switch themes, slick switches with them.

  • Slack in your bar. The leonavas.slack plugin for the Omarchy bar runs on slick and shows what's waiting without opening anything.
  • Slack events in Omarchy. What slick receives (new messages, mentions, reactions, presence, unread counts, pauses) is passed on as events, hooks and a state file, so your own plugins and scripts can react to it and do whatever you want. The bar plugin is built on it.
  • Badges. Red counts unread DMs and group DMs, blue counts channel mentions. Or one combined badge, or a dot without numbers.
  • Hover. The unread conversations with their last message, mention counts and how long ago, plus the reactions to your messages. Click one to jump straight to it in slick.
  • Reactions float up. When someone reacts to your message, the emoji rises out of the icon, the workspace's own emoji included.
  • Click to show or hide. slick lives in Omarchy's scratchpad (Super+S) and keeps running and counting while hidden. Middle click quits it.
  • Right click. Launch on Login, Launch in Foreground, Open in Scratchpad, Group notifications, Show reactions, and pause notifications for 30 minutes, 1 or 2 hours, until tomorrow or until a date and time. The pause is Slack's own do-not-disturb, so it follows you to every client.
  • Yours to tune. Icon style, badge colors, what the blue badge counts, how many rows the preview shows, all in the bar's widget settings.

Install the plugin:

omarchy plugin add https://github.com/leonavas/omarchy-slack.git
omarchy plugin enable leonavas.slack --section right

Code, settings and details: github.com/leonavas/omarchy-slack

slick APP09:21

A token or environment variables instead of the sign-in window.

Put one of these in ~/.config/slick/config:

# A) internal Slack app (official): create it from
#    slack-app-manifest.yml, install it, use the User OAuth Token
token = xoxp-…

# B) browser session, no admin needed
token = xoxc-…
cookie = xoxd-…

Or set SLACK_TOKEN and SLACK_COOKIE in the environment. You can also paste the token and the cookie straight into the sign-in screen.

Your session. It's saved to ~/.config/slick/config, readable only by you. The sign-in window keeps its own profile, so signing out of Slack in your browser doesn't sign you out here. If the session expires, slick asks you to sign in again; Ctrl+L opens the same screen any time.

Linux. The script downloads the latest release for your machine, checks its SHA-256 and puts slick in ~/.local/bin, no sudo. SLICK_INSTALL_DIR changes the folder.

Windows. It installs to %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\slick and adds it to your PATH. Use Windows Terminal (or another terminal with colors and images); there's no tray icon on Windows yet.
